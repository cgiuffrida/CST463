1. ASSIGNMENT AND EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL

Research question: Under a fixed training budget, which CNN architecture and
optimization choices improve ImageNet-100 validation accuracy, and why?

Dataset: clane9/imagenet-100, pinned revision
0519dc2f402a3a18c6e57f7913db059215eee25b.
Training images: 126,689. Development-validation images: 5,000. Classes: 100.
Input size: 128 x 128 RGB. Batch size: 64.
Training transforms: RandomResizedCrop(128), random horizontal flip, tensor
conversion, ImageNet mean/std normalization. Validation uses deterministic
resize, center crop, tensor conversion, and the same normalization.

Local hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5060 Ti; notebook reported PyTorch
2.11.0+cu128 on Windows. The reviewed completed local runs use four loader
workers. Sixteen workers were discussed but not used in these saved runs.
More workers prepare data in parallel; they do not add optimizer updates per
epoch, and a larger worker count is not automatically faster.

Each notebook experiment resets the random seed, resets the training-loader
generator, constructs a fresh model, and snapshots its configurations. Runs
are trained from scratch, not continued from previous weights. At batch size
64 there are 1,980 training batches per epoch. Results are saved as unique JSON
files containing model/run settings, dataset identity, parameter count, and
per-epoch loss, accuracy, and training-plus-validation time.

The final benchmark trains from new weights for 90 epochs under multiple
instructor-selected seeds, with a two-hour safety limit. Its score averages
private-evaluation accuracy over the final five epochs and across seeds.
Our final-five development-validation average is a relevant selection metric,
but is not the instructor's private score or a multi-seed estimate.
The trial service allows four submissions, each capped at 15 epochs and
approximately 30 minutes. One trial result has been reviewed in this chat.

Allowed work: student-written PyTorch architectures and training choices,
with public method contracts preserved. No pretrained weights, imported
model-zoo architectures, external datasets, or downloaded weights were used.
The instructor discourages changing train_model because the service replaces
it. Final benchmark deliverables are student_code.py and benchmark-config.yaml;
the report is compiled to report.pdf. Follow the course citation/collaboration
policy when using this summary and external design references.

A. Add a fifth block: run_14 versus run_15

Shared settings: seed 1, 15 epochs, AdamW, LR 0.003, weight decay 0.0001,
dropout 0, batch size 64, four workers, one LR reduction by factor 0.3 after
epoch 12 (0.003 to 0.0009, effective for epoch 13).

run_15 channels: [32,64,256,512,1024].
Final accuracy improved by 7.28 percentage points, for 3.34 additional minutes.
run_15 best accuracy: 69.86% at epoch 13.
Interpretation: adding this particular block helped. It jointly adds depth,
parameters, and another pooling operation, so it does not isolate depth alone.

B. Repeat the five-block model: run_16

Same configuration as run_15, but seed 0. Final accuracy: 70.44%.
The two-seed mean final accuracy is 70.08%, with a 0.72-point difference.
This supports reproducibility of the five-block model around 70% at this
budget, but two seeds do not establish its full variability. A matched seed-0
four-block run with decay after epoch 12 was not completed in this chat.

C. Add one convolution without another pooling stage: run_17

Actual saved seed is 0, so compare to run_16, not run_15. The new convolution
is inserted AFTER the THIRD MaxPool2d, before the original fourth block:
Conv2d(256,256,kernel_size=3,padding=1), BatchNorm2d(256), ReLU.
It processes 16 x 16 feature maps and adds no pooling operation.
The initial suggestion to place it before that pool was revised before the
implemented experiment, to reduce computation. Do not describe it as being
before the third pool in the report.

Implementation uses an optional extra_conv constructor argument and a block
loop numbered with enumerate(channels, start=1). The extra layers are appended
after pooling when extra_conv is true and block_number == 3.
Added parameters: 590,592. Total: 6,763,748.
Final accuracy improved by 0.56 points; final validation loss improved from
1.1452 to 1.1053. Runtime rose about 7.2%.
Interpretation: a modest promising improvement, not independently replicated.
The extra layer initially learned more slowly and caught up later; adding
depth was not guaranteed to help under a fixed epoch budget.

D. Full-budget training with cosine decay: run_18

Retained the extra-convolution architecture, seed 0, dropout 0, and weight
decay 0.0001. Changed the training budget to 90 epochs and replaced the one-time
step reduction with cosine decay from LR 0.003 toward 0.00003 over 90 epochs.
Final accuracy: 74.64%. Final-five average: 74.644%.
Best accuracy: 74.84% at epoch 79. Final training accuracy: 87.56%.
Lowest validation loss: 1.1316 at epoch 33; final validation loss: 1.2361.
Training loss continued to decline while validation loss generally worsened
after its minimum. Validation accuracy still rose slowly late in training.
Interpretation: evidence of increasing overfitting/overconfidence motivated
regularization. The loss/accuracy pattern is consistent with increasingly
confident errors, but prediction-level evidence was not collected to prove it.
Because BOTH duration and schedule changed from run_17, this comparison does
not isolate the effect of cosine scheduling.

E. Combined regularization: run_19 — CURRENT SELECTED CONFIGURATION

Same architecture, seed, 90-epoch cosine schedule, and data settings as run_18.
Changed dropout 0.0 -> 0.2 AND AdamW weight decay 0.0001 -> 0.001.
Final accuracy: 76.78%. Final-five average: 76.736%.
Best accuracy: 77.00% at epoch 83. Final training accuracy: 82.81%.
Final validation loss: 0.9978. Lowest validation loss: 0.9762 at epoch 63.
Gains over run_18: +2.14 points final accuracy; +2.092 points final-five mean.
Parameter count unchanged; runtime approximately unchanged.

Interpretation: the combined regularization change improved validation
performance while reducing training accuracy. Validation loss also became
substantially lower and more stable. We cannot attribute the gain to dropout
alone or weight decay alone. Dropout is active in training and disabled in
evaluation, and training uses augmentation; the observed train/validation gap
is not a controlled measurement of generalization error.

For the comparison figure, replace "Higher dropout model" with something like
"Dropout 0.2 + weight decay 0.001". Existing comparison_013.png reports FINAL
accuracy (74.64% versus 76.78%), not final-five means; label any new figure or

5. CURRENT ARCHITECTURE, CONFIGURATION, AND CODE STATUS

Architecture for run_19:
- Conv 3->32, BN, ReLU, MaxPool: spatial size 128->64.
- Conv 32->64, BN, ReLU, MaxPool: 64->32.
- Conv 64->256, BN, ReLU, MaxPool: 32->16.
- Extra Conv 256->256, BN, ReLU: remains 16 x 16.
- Conv 256->512, BN, ReLU, MaxPool: 16->8.
- Conv 512->1024, BN, ReLU, MaxPool: 8->4.
- Adaptive average pool to 1 x 1, flatten, dropout 0.2, linear 1024->100.
All convolutions are 3 x 3, stride 1, padding 1, with bias as in the code.
There are six convolutions, six batch-normalization layers, and five max pools.
The forward method returns logits; loss is ordinary mean cross-entropy.
No label smoothing, mixup, pretrained model, or residual architecture was added.

7. HOW TO USE THIS EVIDENCE IN THE REPORT

Possible central argument:
Early experiments improved feature capacity and optimization speed. Longer
training exposed a generalization problem that was less apparent at 15 epochs.
A combined dropout/weight-decay change improved the final-five validation
average from 74.644% to 76.736% without increasing model size. Run_19 is therefore
the current selected configuration, pending the final weight-decay experiment.

Choose 3-5 focused comparisons rather than narrating every exploratory run:
- Earlier replicated width comparison (seeds 0 and 1; 15 epochs).
- Fourth versus fifth block (run_14 versus run_15; seed 1; 15 epochs).
- Extra convolution (run_16 versus run_17; seed 0; 15 epochs).
- Combined regularization (run_18 versus run_19; seed 0; 90 epochs).
- Optional final weight-decay comparison (run_19 versus run_20) once completed.
Include the original baseline from the earlier summary and saved results.
Describe the 15-to-90-epoch/cosine transition as context, not an isolated
scheduler ablation. Equal epochs are not equal runtime; report both.

Key limitations and claims to avoid:
- Run_19's 90-epoch result currently has one seed; no claim of statistical
  significance or guaranteed improvement across instructor seeds is warranted.
- Multiple experiments reused development-validation data. It is a selection
  set, not an untouched estimate of private-evaluation performance.
- The gain from run_18 to run_19 belongs to a bundled regularization change.
- A lower training/validation gap is not itself the benchmark objective.
- More layers, more regularization, more workers, or larger learning rates do
  not guarantee improvements.
- The 77.00% peak is not the selection metric; use the final-five average.
- Run_19 is best observed among completed configurations, not a proven optimum.
- Instructor runtime projections are estimates; the latest architecture and
  regularization combination has not completed a 90-epoch instructor run.

Before benchmark results, a defensible expectation is that the selected model
will outperform the modest original baseline, with local development accuracy
around 76-77% for the tested seed. Do not promise that exact private score.
